## The picks (my recommendation first in each)
- **a. Publish.** Recommend **a1** (confirm dialog, both buttons stay): smallest build, keeps the create-and-publish path P2a's walk already uses, and the dialog names what goes public, what starts and the one-way warning. **a2** (draft-first, review in place) is spec 003 FR-003's own shape and avoids a modal, but removes Publish from the create form and so changes the §8 acceptance walk. **a3** (save and review, with a public-card preview) is the richest and the biggest; the preview can drift from the real public page. All three end on the same Published banner plus "What to do next" (share link, registrations, when check-in opens).
- **b. Registrations and Check-in.** Recommend **b2** (toolbar buttons and a link across): shortest path to O6 (no "Roster" landing on Check-in, matching names). **b1** (one five-place tab bar on every event page) is the spec's end state and answers "where am I" best, but is the largest build and at 375 px only three of five tabs are visible, Edit and Analytics need a sideways scroll [X, seen in the render]. **b3** (two-way switch on the two pages only) is the middle; it is replaced if b1 comes later.
- **c. Save confirmation.** Recommend **c1** (status banner above the form, survives the redirect): D15-compliant (result-driven, polite live region, no timer), smallest change, the form already has the banner for edits. **c2** (on the button) is easy to miss; **c3** (fixed bottom bar) is the most legible and the most invasive (padding on every form page, competes with the phone keyboard). Whichever wins also covers the plain "Save changes" on later edits; the create-to-edit redirect needs a signal (a query flag or flash value) in every option.
- **d. Container style (a skill conflict, not one of your three surfaces).** Recommend **d1** (as the product is built today). **d2** (double shell, from high-end-visual-design) and **d3** (no lines, tone steps, from design-taste-frontend) are drawn so you can decide; d2 adds a second container style next to every existing screen, d3 weakens the panel edge in forced-colours mode. Applies to new MVP surfaces only.
